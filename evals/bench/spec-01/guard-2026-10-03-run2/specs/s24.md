# Spec: customer self-service ticket reopen

Status: **draft, awaiting review** — written while the requester was away; every
open question below is answered with a recommendation rather than left
pending. Do not build from this until the recommendations are confirmed or
corrected.

## 1. Restatement

**Request, in the requester's words:** "Let a customer reopen a closed ticket
from the portal within 14 days of it being closed, and notify the assigned
agent when they do."

**Technical reading:** add a new portal-facing operation that moves a
`closed` ticket back to `open` when the acting customer's organisation owns
it and fewer than 14 days have passed since `closed_at`, then queues a
notification to the ticket's assignee (if any) the same way the existing
agent-side reopen does. This is new behavior, not a fix: today `closed` is
documented and enforced as final (`docs/glossary.md:12`, `src/portal.js:24`,
`src/tickets.js:38-40`).

## 2. Current behavior (verified against live code)

- Status model: `open → resolved → closed`, plus `pending`, defined in
  `src/tickets.js:4`.
- `tickets.reopenTicket(store, id, agentId)` (`src/tickets.js:38-44`) is
  **agent-only** and only accepts a `resolved` ticket. It is pinned by exact
  error-message assertions in `test/tickets.test.js:34-41` — it must not
  change shape.
- `portal.reply(store, customer, id, body)` (`src/portal.js:21-28`) throws on
  any `closed` ticket and reopens a `resolved`/`pending` one as a side
  effect of answering. It does not touch `closed`.
- Nightly jobs: `closeResolved` (`src/jobs/close-resolved.js`) closes a
  quiet resolved ticket after 3 days; `purgeClosed`
  (`src/jobs/purge-closed.js:10-19`) deletes a closed ticket's messages 7
  days after `closed_at` and sets `purged: true`, which is a **one-way
  latch** (`if (t.purged) continue`, line 13) — it never clears itself.
- `notify.notifyAgent(store, agentId, event)` (`src/notify.js:6-9`) throws if
  `agentId` is falsy; the agent-side reopen already guards this by only
  notifying `if (t.assignee_id && ...)` (`src/tickets.js:42`).
- Org scoping for everything a customer touches is by `org_id` only, not
  `customer_id` — `portal.getMyTicket` (`src/portal.js:10-14`) lets any
  customer in the organisation see/act on any ticket in that organisation.
  `reply` and `openTicket` follow the same rule.

## 3. Open questions, answered by recommendation

1. **Is the 14-day window inclusive or exclusive of day 14?**
   Recommend exclusive (`now - closed_at < 14 days`), matching the existing
   boundary style in `purgeClosed` (`src/jobs/purge-closed.js:14`, which uses
   strict `<` the same way). At exactly 14 days, reopening is refused.

2. **Does reopening require the customer to say anything, or is a bare
   status flip enough?**
   Recommend requiring a non-empty message, written to the ticket as the
   customer's first message after reopening. Rationale: `portal.reply`
   already requires a `body` for every other customer action
   (`src/portal.js:21`), and notifying an agent with zero context defeats
   the point of the notification.

3. **Is this the same function as the agent-only `reopenTicket`, extended to
   accept `closed`, or a new function?**
   Recommend a new function, `tickets.reopenByCustomer`. `reopenTicket` is
   pinned by exact-message tests for "resolved-only" behavior
   (`test/tickets.test.js:34-41`); widening its accepted statuses would
   break that contract and blur an agent action with a customer action that
   has different rules (window, message requirement, actor identity).

4. **Who can reopen — only the ticket's original customer, or anyone in the
   same organisation?**
   Recommend anyone in the organisation, for consistency with every other
   portal action (`getMyTicket`, `reply`, `openTicket` all scope by `org_id`
   only). Introducing a stricter rule just for reopen would be a new,
   undocumented inconsistency.

5. **What event type does the agent notification use?**
   Recommend reusing the existing `ticket_reopened` event type with
   `by: customer:<id>` (the acting customer's id, not necessarily the
   ticket's original `customer_id`, since any org member may act). This
   mirrors how the agent-side reopen already encodes the actor
   (`src/tickets.js:42`, `by: agent:${agentId}`) rather than inventing a
   second event type for the same fact ("this ticket came back to open").

6. **What if `assignee_id` is null?**
   Recommend skip silently, no notification attempted. `notifyAgent` throws
   on a falsy `agentId` (`src/notify.js:7`), and the agent path already
   avoids this by checking `t.assignee_id` first
   (`src/tickets.js:42`); the customer path must do the same.

7. **A ticket can be purged (messages deleted, `purged: true`) as early as
   7 days after closing — 7 days inside the 14-day reopen window. Can a
   purged ticket still be reopened?**
   Recommend yes, with no special case. `purgeClosed` already documents that
   "the ticket row stays as a summary" after purge
   (`src/jobs/purge-closed.js:3`); reopening adds a fresh message and the
   conversation continues from there. The pre-purge history is gone, which
   is the purge job's documented, legal-driven behavior, not something this
   feature should work around.

8. **Must reopening clear the `purged` flag?**
   Recommend yes, unconditionally, every time a ticket is reopened by a
   customer — even if the ticket was never purged (setting `false` on an
   already-`false` row is harmless). Without this, a ticket that reopens,
   gets answered, closes again, and reaches day 7 of its *second* closed
   period is skipped forever by `purgeClosed`'s `if (t.purged) continue`
   latch (`src/jobs/purge-closed.js:13`), silently keeping a second round of
   closed-ticket messages past the 7-day retention limit. This is the one
   item in this spec that is **not a style choice**: getting it wrong is a
   retention-policy violation, not just a bug. See acceptance criterion 6.

9. **Does this change the SLA due time, add a reopen counter, or notify the
   customer themselves?**
   Recommend no to all three — out of scope for v1. Nothing in the request
   asks for it, and `createTicket` already fixes `sla_due_at` once at
   creation (`src/tickets.js:16`) with no other code that revisits it.

10. **What should `portal.reopen` return?**
    Recommend the full ticket-with-messages view, i.e. the same shape as
    `getMyTicket` and what `portal.reply` returns
    (`src/portal.js:27`), rather than the raw store row, so a caller doesn't
    need a second fetch.

## 4. Design

### `src/tickets.js`

- Add `const DAY = 24 * HOUR;` next to the existing `HOUR` constant
  (`src/tickets.js:6`). `HOUR` is already shared between this file and the
  job files; the job files each currently redefine their own `DAY` locally
  (`src/jobs/close-resolved.js:6`, `src/jobs/purge-closed.js:8`) — this spec
  does not touch those, only avoids adding a third inline copy of the
  literal.
- Add:

  ```js
  function reopenByCustomer(store, id, message, { now = new Date(), actorCustomerId } = {}) {
    const t = mustGet(store, id);
    if (t.status !== STATUS.CLOSED) throw new Error(`cannot reopen a ${t.status} ticket`);
    if (now.getTime() - new Date(t.closed_at).getTime() >= 14 * DAY) {
      throw new Error('this ticket closed more than 14 days ago; please open a new one');
    }
    if (!message || !message.trim()) throw new Error('reopening a ticket needs a message');
    const updated = store.updateTicket(id, {
      status: STATUS.OPEN,
      resolved_at: null,
      closed_at: null,
      purged: false,
    });
    store.addMessage(id, { from: 'customer', body: message, at: now.toISOString() });
    if (t.assignee_id) {
      const by = `customer:${actorCustomerId != null ? actorCustomerId : t.customer_id}`;
      notifyAgent(store, t.assignee_id, { type: 'ticket_reopened', ticketId: id, by });
    }
    return updated;
  }
  ```

  `actorCustomerId` is optional and defaults to the ticket's own
  `customer_id` when omitted, so `by` is always correct even if a caller
  doesn't pass it. The portal wrapper below always passes it, since the
  acting customer (`customer.id`) may differ from the ticket's original
  opener (`t.customer_id`) — see open question 5.

- Export `reopenByCustomer` alongside the existing exports
  (`src/tickets.js:52`).
- `reopenTicket` (agent-only) is **untouched**.

### `src/portal.js`

- Import `reopenByCustomer` alongside `STATUS, createTicket`
  (`src/portal.js:4`).
- Add:

  ```js
  function reopen(store, customer, id, message, { now = new Date() } = {}) {
    const t = getMyTicket(store, customer, id);
    if (!t) return null;
    reopenByCustomer(store, id, message, { now, actorCustomerId: customer.id });
    return getMyTicket(store, customer, id);
  }
  ```

  `customer.id` is passed as `actorCustomerId` so the notification's
  `by: customer:<id>` reflects who actually reopened it, not necessarily
  the ticket's original opener — see open question 5. The agent-only call
  path is unaffected since it never calls `reopenByCustomer` at all.
- Export `reopen` alongside the existing exports (`src/portal.js:30`).
- `reply`, `openTicket`, `getMyTicket`, `listMyTickets` are **untouched**.

### `docs/glossary.md`

- The **closed** bullet (`docs/glossary.md:12`) currently reads "final...
  never changed again. If the customer comes back, they start a new
  ticket." Update to note the 14-day reopen exception, e.g.: "closed:
  final, unless the customer reopens it from the portal within 14 days of
  closing — after that window, or past 14 days, they start a new ticket."

### `test/tickets.test.js`

New tests to add (not a rewrite of existing ones):

- Reopen within the window succeeds and clears `resolved_at`/`closed_at`.
- Reopen at/after 14 days throws and leaves the ticket closed.
- Reopen with an empty/whitespace message throws.
- Reopen on a non-closed ticket (open, pending, resolved) throws.
- Reopen notifies the assignee with `ticket_reopened`/`by: customer:<id>`;
  reopen on an unassigned ticket does not throw and queues nothing.
- **Regression for the purge latch**: close → purge (day 11, as in the
  existing purge test) → reopen (still within 14 days of the original
  close) → close again → purge again at day 11 of the *second* close.
  Assert the second purge actually deletes the second round of messages
  (i.e. `purged` was reset to `false` by the reopen).
- `portal.reopen` returns `null` for a ticket outside the customer's
  organisation, mirroring the existing `getMyTicket` scoping test
  (`test/tickets.test.js:46`).
- The existing test "only a resolved ticket can be reopened"
  (`test/tickets.test.js:34-41`) still passes unmodified — confirms
  `reopenTicket` (agent path) is untouched.

## 5. Edge cases

1. **Ticket already purged, still inside the 14-day window** (closed at day
   0, purged at day 7, customer reopens at day 10): allowed; old messages
   stay deleted, the reopen message is the start of the new conversation.
   See open question 7.
2. **Reopen → close → purge, second cycle**: the `purged` flag must be
   reset on reopen or the second cycle's messages are never purged — a
   retention-policy miss. See open question 8 and acceptance criterion 6.
3. **Unassigned ticket (`assignee_id` is `null`)**: reopen must not call
   `notifyAgent` (it throws on a falsy agent id) — skip the notification,
   do not fail the reopen.
4. **Empty or whitespace-only message**: rejected before any state changes
   (status, dates, and `purged` are all left as they were).
5. **Ticket belongs to a different organisation than the acting customer**:
   `portal.reopen` returns `null`, exactly like `getMyTicket`, revealing
   nothing about the ticket's existence or status.
6. **Concurrent reopen attempts on the same ticket**: not specifically
   handled — the in-memory store (`src/store.js`) has no locking anywhere
   else in the codebase either, so this is consistent with the existing
   concurrency model, not a regression. Out of scope for this feature.

## 6. Acceptance criteria

1. `tickets.reopenByCustomer` on a `closed` ticket fewer than 14 days past
   `closed_at`, with a non-empty message, sets `status: 'open'`,
   `resolved_at: null`, `closed_at: null`, and appends the message.
2. The same call at or after 14 days past `closed_at` throws and leaves the
   ticket's status, dates, and messages unchanged.
3. The same call with an empty or whitespace-only message throws and
   changes nothing.
4. The same call on a ticket whose status is `open`, `pending`, or
   `resolved` throws `cannot reopen a <status> ticket` and changes nothing.
5. Reopening a ticket with a non-null `assignee_id` queues exactly one
   `notify` entry: `{ to: 'agent:<assignee_id>', event: { type:
   'ticket_reopened', ticketId, by: 'customer:<id>' } }`. Reopening an
   unassigned ticket queues nothing and does not throw.
6. **Purge-latch regression**: a ticket that is closed, purged (messages
   deleted, `purged: true`), reopened, answered, closed again, and purged
   again (now past 7 days of its *second* close) has its second round of
   messages deleted by the second `purgeClosed` run. This fails today's
   naive "just flip status back to open" approach if `purged` is left
   `true`, and that is the point of the test.
7. `portal.reopen` returns the full ticket-with-messages view
   (`getMyTicket` shape) on success, and `null` when the ticket belongs to
   a different organisation than the acting customer.
8. The existing agent-only `reopenTicket` and its pinned tests
   (`test/tickets.test.js:34-41`) are unchanged and still pass.

## 7. Out of scope (v1)

- SLA due-time reset on reopen.
- A limit on how many times a ticket can be reopened.
- Notifying the customer themselves (only the assigned agent, per the
  request).
- Any portal UI/frontend work — this spec is the backend operation only.
- A new notify event type — reuses `ticket_reopened`.
- Changing `portal.reply`'s behavior on closed tickets — reopen is a
  distinct, deliberate customer action, not a side effect of replying.
