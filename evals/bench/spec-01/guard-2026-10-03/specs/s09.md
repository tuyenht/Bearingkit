# Spec: customer self-service ticket reopen

Status: **Draft — awaiting owner sign-off** (written 2026-10-03 while owner was unavailable; see "Questions answered by recommendation" below — nothing in this document has been implemented or built yet)

## 1. Restatement

**In the customer's words:** let a customer reopen their own closed ticket from the portal, as long as it's been less than 14 days since it closed, and let the agent who's on the ticket know it came back.

**Technical reading:** add a new customer-facing portal action that transitions a ticket from `closed` to `open` when `now - closed_at < 14 days`, records the customer's follow-up message, clears the fields that marked it closed, and enqueues a notification to the ticket's assignee (if any). This is a new capability, not a change to the existing agent-side reopen (`tickets.js:38-44`, resolved→open only) or to `portal.reply()` (`portal.js:21-28`, which still refuses closed tickets outright).

These two readings match; no gap to flag.

## 2. The conflict this creates — and the recommended resolution

`docs/glossary.md:12` currently says:

> **closed**: final. A closed ticket is never changed again. If the customer comes back, they start a new ticket.

This request directly contradicts that sentence. It has to change, or the shipped behavior and the documented word meaning disagree.

**Recommendation (confidence: high):** narrow the glossary line to carry the exception explicitly, rather than deleting the "final" framing wholesale — the agent-side rules (`assignTicket`, `resolveTicket` both still refuse any closed ticket) are unaffected and should stay described as final for everyone except this one customer-initiated, time-boxed path:

> **closed**: final for agents — a closed ticket cannot be assigned, resolved, or answered by staff. The customer may reopen it themselves from the portal within 14 days of closing; after that window, or for any agent-side action, closed is final and they start a new ticket.

## 3. Classification

**COUNCIL**, not ACT. Three independent triggers from the Autonomy Gate apply, any one of which would be enough:

- **Module contract change** — it rewrites what `closed` is allowed to mean, a contract `tickets.js`, `portal.js`, and the glossary all currently agree on.
- **Multi-module impact** — touches the ticket state machine (`tickets.js`), the portal surface (`portal.js`), notification (`notify.js`), and — critically — interacts with the nightly data-deletion job (`src/jobs/purge-closed.js`), none of which is a 3-file ACT change.
- **Data deletion** — `purge-closed.js` is a legally-motivated retention control (`purge-closed.js:2-4`, "Legal asked for this"). This feature changes when and whether purged data gets purged again. That makes it a **hot path**: it should get an independent review before this ships, same as auth or payments would.

This spec is written and the design decisions below are **recommendations only** — nothing proceeds to `bk-plan`/`bk-build` until the owner accepts or amends the verdict in §4.

## 4. Council

```
## Council: how should a customer-initiated reopen interact with the 7-day message purge?
Roles: data retention & compliance, support operations, engineering

### Options
- data retention & compliance: block reopening entirely once a ticket's messages have been purged (days 7-14 of the window), so no code path ever needs to "un-purge." Cost: customers lose the reopen right precisely in the back half of the 14-day window they were promised, which is confusing and shrinks the promised window to 7 days in practice. Risk: support complaints, and it quietly breaks the stated "14 days" promise.
- support operations: allow reopening through day 14 regardless of purge state, since the ticket row (subject, dates, org) is kept for reporting anyway and the customer's new message is what matters going forward, not the old thread. Cost: the reopened ticket's history shows a gap (old messages gone, new ones start fresh) — needs a small portal-UI note. Risk: none to data retention, since purge only ever deletes messages already past their legal keep-window; it never deletes the ticket itself.
- engineering: allow reopening through day 14 (agree with support operations), but the reopen must reset `purged: false` on the ticket row every time, unconditionally. Cost: one extra field in the `updateTicket` patch. Risk if skipped: `purge-closed.js:13` ("`if (t.purged) continue`") would permanently skip this ticket on every future nightly run, so messages added after a reopen-then-recloses cycle would never be purged — conversation text would be retained past the legal 7-day limit, silently. This is the gotcha this spec exists to catch.

### Conflicts
Compliance's "just block it" option is the only one that avoids touching the purge job at all, but it breaks the literal 14-day promise the customer-facing request makes, for no compliance benefit (the ticket row was never going to be deleted either way). Support operations and engineering agree on allowing the full window; engineering adds the one concrete safeguard that makes that safe.

### Verdict
Allow reopening through the full 14 days independent of purge state, and make the reopen path **always** write `purged: false`, whatever its current value, as part of the same update that clears `closed_at`. This keeps the promised window intact, costs nothing in compliance risk (the retention clock restarts correctly), and the fix is one field.

### Rejected
- blocking reopen once purged: breaks the stated 14-day promise for the back half of the window, with no corresponding compliance gain.

### Priority matrix
| Item | Impact | Effort | Order |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reset `purged: false` on reopen | High (compliance correctness) | Low | 1st — not optional |
| Glossary wording update | Medium (prevents future confusion) | Low | with the code change |
| Portal UI note on gapped history | Low | Low | can follow in a later pass |
```

## 5. Questions answered by recommendation

The owner is away until tomorrow; per standing preference, each question is answered inline rather than left open. Anything marked below should be treated as **proposed**, not decided, until reviewed.

1. **Does an agent also gain the ability to reopen a closed ticket, or is this customer-only?**
   Recommendation: customer-only, as asked. Agent-side `assignTicket`/`resolveTicket` keep throwing on a closed ticket exactly as today (`tickets.js:27`, `tickets.js:33`). *(confidence: high — matches the literal request, no reason to expand scope)*

2. **Does reopening require the customer to submit a message, or can they reopen with no content?**
   Recommendation: the action takes a message body and records it the same way `portal.reply()` does (`portal.js:25`), so the agent sees *why* it came back in the thread. No new emptiness validation beyond what `reply()` already does (it doesn't check either) — consistent with the existing code's leniency rather than inventing a stricter rule here. *(confidence: high)*

3. **Is reopening blocked once the ticket's messages have already been purged (days 7–14 of the window)?**
   Recommendation: no — see the Council verdict above. Reopening is allowed through day 14 regardless of purge state, and the reopen must reset `purged: false`. *(confidence: high — this is the load-bearing decision of this spec)*

4. **What exactly resets when a closed ticket reopens — does `closed_at` get cleared?**
   Recommendation: yes. Mirror the existing agent-side `reopenTicket`, which clears `resolved_at: null` on reopen (`tickets.js:41`) — clear `closed_at: null` the same way, since it's no longer accurate and nothing in this request asks for a closure-history log. *(confidence: medium — if reporting ever wants a full closure history, that's a separate, later ask)*

5. **Who gets notified if the ticket had no assignee when it closed?**
   Recommendation: nobody — `notify.js:7` already throws if called with no agent id, so skip the call entirely when `assignee_id` is null, the same guard shape the existing agent-reopen path uses (`tickets.js:42`, `if (t.assignee_id && ...)`). The ticket simply reopens into the unassigned queue. *(confidence: high)*

6. **Exact boundary: is a ticket closed exactly 14×24h ago still reopenable?**
   Recommendation: no — reject once elapsed time is `>= 14 days`, allow while `< 14 days`, matching the same `<` convention `purge-closed.js:14` already uses for its own day-boundary check. *(confidence: medium — no existing customer-facing copy pins this either way, but matching the codebase's own convention beats inventing a new one)*

7. **Is there a cap on how many times one ticket can be reopened this way, or any abuse/rate-limit protection?**
   Recommendation: no cap for v1 — out of scope (YAGNI; nothing in the request implies repeat-reopen abuse is a known problem). Flag as a future follow-up only if support reports it. *(confidence: medium)*

8. **New notification event name?**
   Recommendation: `ticket_reopened_by_customer`, distinct from the existing agent-side `ticket_reopened` (`tickets.js:42`), so the mail worker and any future analytics can tell the two apart. `by` field reads `` `customer:${customer.id}` ``, mirroring the existing `` `agent:${agentId}` `` shape (`tickets.js:42`). *(confidence: high)*

## 6. Edge cases

1. **Ticket had no assignee when closed.** Skip the notification (see Q5); ticket reopens into the unassigned queue with `assignee_id` unchanged (`null`).
2. **Ticket's messages were already purged (closed 7–13 days ago).** Reopen is still allowed; the thread simply starts with only the new message. `purged` resets to `false` regardless of its current value (the Council verdict, §4) — this is the one non-negotiable requirement in this spec.
3. **Reopen attempted at or past the 14-day boundary.** Reject with an error (e.g. `this ticket closed more than 14 days ago; please open a new one`, matching the tone of the existing `portal.js:24` message). No state changes.
4. **Reopen attempted on a ticket belonging to a different organisation.** Already handled for free: `getMyTicket` (`portal.js:10-14`) returns `null` for a cross-org id, same as every other portal function; the new action returns `null`/404 the same way, no new check needed.
5. **Reopen attempted on a ticket that isn't closed** (open, pending, or resolved). Reject with a distinct error from the agent-side one (`tickets.js:40`), so logs/tests can tell which code path rejected it — e.g. `cannot reopen a ${t.status} ticket this way`.
6. **Ticket reopened, closed again, then reopened a second time.** The 14-day window is recalculated from the *most recent* `closed_at` each time, not the original closure — every closure starts its own fresh customer-reopen window.
7. **Double-submit / concurrency** (customer clicks "Reopen" twice quickly). The second call re-reads the ticket, sees `status` is already `open`, and falls into edge case 5's rejection — no duplicate notification is enqueued, since `notifyAgent` is only called once per successful transition.

## 7. Requirements (acceptance criteria)

A new portal function, e.g. `portal.reopenClosedTicket(store, customer, id, body, { now })`:

- **AC1:** Given a ticket in the caller's own org, `status: closed`, `closed_at` less than 14 days before `now` → the call succeeds, returns the ticket with `status: open`, `resolved_at: null`, `closed_at: null`, `purged: false`, and the new message appended to its thread.
- **AC2:** Given the same, but `closed_at` is 14 or more days before `now` → the call throws and no field on the ticket changes.
- **AC3:** Given a ticket with `status` other than `closed` → the call throws a distinct error from AC2's, and no field changes.
- **AC4:** Given a ticket belonging to a different org than the caller → the call returns `null` (not found), consistent with `getMyTicket`.
- **AC5:** Given a closed ticket with `purged: true` and `closed_at` within the 14-day window → the call succeeds and the resulting ticket has `purged: false`.
- **AC6:** Given a closed ticket with a non-null `assignee_id` → exactly one notification is queued, `event.type === 'ticket_reopened_by_customer'`, `to === `agent:${assignee_id}``.
- **AC7:** Given a closed ticket with `assignee_id: null` → the call succeeds and no notification is queued (and does not throw inside `notifyAgent`).
- **AC8 (docs):** `docs/glossary.md`'s `closed` entry reflects the exception (§2) rather than stating closed tickets are unconditionally final.

## 9. Out of scope (v1)

- Agent-initiated reopen of a closed ticket (unchanged — stays impossible, §5.1).
- A UI affordance distinguishing "reopened with gapped history" from "reopened with full history" — a later portal-UI pass if support asks for it.
- Rate limiting / abuse prevention on repeat reopens (§5.7).
- A closure-history log (multiple `closed_at` values over a ticket's life) — only the latest is tracked, matching how `resolved_at` already behaves.
