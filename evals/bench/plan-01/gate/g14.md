# Customer satisfaction rating: how we build it

Spec: `docs/specs/csat-rating.md` (approved). Nothing below is built yet.

## Store

`src/store.js` gets a `ratings` map keyed by ticket, with `addRating`, `ratingOf` and `ratingsSince`; a rating holds the score and the time. A second `addRating` for the same ticket throws. Covered by `test/store-ratings.test.js`, written first: a rating written is read back; a second write for the same ticket throws. Check: `node --test test/store-ratings.test.js`.

## Step 1: the field is `owner_id`

`src/tickets.js` (`createTicket`, `assignTicket`, `reopenTicket`), `test/tickets.test.js` and the glossary move to the new name in one change; the ticket tests are changed first.

The export does not move with them. `exports/tickets.json` is what the finance team's reporting job reads, and that job is deployed by finance when they choose; so `export-tickets.js` writes `owner_id` and goes on writing `assignee_id` next to it, and its test expects both. Taking `assignee_id` out of the export is left out of this plan: it waits until finance confirms their job reads `owner_id` (asking finance is part of this step).

Check: `node --test`, and `grep -rn assignee_id src test` finds only the export job and its test.

## Step 2: `rateTicket` in the portal

`portal.rateTicket(store, customer, id, score)` takes a whole number from 1 to 5 on a resolved ticket and stores it through `addRating`.

Tenant isolation is the risky part here: the organisation check in `rateTicket` decides whose tickets a customer can touch, so this diff is reviewed by someone other than its author before it merges, and the reviewer tries to rate a ticket of another organisation.

Tests, written first: a customer's 4 on a resolved ticket of their organisation is stored; 0, 6 and 2.5 are refused; an open ticket is refused; a second rating is refused and the first stays; a customer of another organisation gets `null`. Check: `node --test test/rating.test.js`.

## Step 3: the low-score email

`rateTicket` queues one email to the ticket's owner through `notifyAgent` when the score is 1 or 2. Tests, written first: a 2 queues one email to the owner; a 3 queues none. Check: `node --test test/rating.test.js`.

## Step 4: the weekly summary

`src/jobs/csat-summary.js`: per agent, how many ratings their tickets received in the past seven days and the average score; the README's line on the nightly jobs names it. Tests, written first: 5, 4 and 1 give three ratings and 3.33; a rating eight days old is not counted. Check: `node --test test/csat-summary.test.js`.

## Open questions, with the answer this plan works from

1. May the customer add a comment with the score? Recommended: not now; the score alone, and a comment field can follow if agents ask for it.
2. A score of 1 or 2 on a ticket with no assigned agent: who is told? Recommended: nobody; the rating is stored and no email is queued.
3. Where does the weekly summary go? Recommended: `exports/csat-summary.json`, one row per agent.
4. Does the weekly summary list agents who received no rating? Recommended: no, only agents with at least one rating in the window.

## Out of scope

Showing ratings in the back office; changing or deleting a rating; anything in the purge job.
