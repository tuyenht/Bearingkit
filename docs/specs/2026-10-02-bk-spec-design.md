**The rules file.** `evals/bench/spec-01/rules.json`, committed in step 1, its SHA-256 written here before step 2, holds for every item the exact terms. A **block** is one paragraph, one list item, one table row or one heading with the text up to the next blank line. A hazard holds when one block carries all its term groups (for H2, for instance: a word of the 14-day window, and "purge" or "deleted" or "7 days"). An **open question** is (a) a top-level list item or a table body row inside a section whose heading holds "question" (any case), or a paragraph inside such a section that holds a "?"; or (b) outside such a section, a top-level list item, table body row or paragraph that ends with "?". Never an open question: a heading; a table's header and separator rows; a nested list item; a paragraph with no "?" inside a question section (an introduction); and any block that begins with "Recommend", "Default", "Answer" or "→" (the answer to the question above it). It is **numbered** when it starts with a number or a `Q<n>` label, and **carries a recommendation** when it or the block after it holds "recommend" or "→" or "default". A decoy holds when an open question carries the decoy's terms and none of the decision terms of H1, H5 or H6 (so "reuse `reopenTicket` or write a new one?" is a decision, not D3); this exemption is deliberate and errs toward counting fewer decoys. Every hazard, every decoy, G1 and G2 count only where O1 holds; a session with no file scores H = 0, D = 0, G1 and G2 false. The request leaves open **at least five decisions** (what "closed" is to mean here, the window against the purge, the SLA reset, the unassigned ticket, a limit on how many times a ticket may be reopened), so a spec that asks everything it could exceeds the cap; a spec that asks little still passes G2 without having applied the cap, which G2 cannot tell apart and the count of open questions per spec, reported, shows.

**The scorer is tried before any session** on hand-written specs: one passing all seven hazards with four numbered questions; seven each missing one hazard; one naive spec that restates the request; one that asks the three decoy facts; one with six unnumbered questions; one whose decision questions use the decoys' words; and by mutation (each condition dropped in turn), as `evals/bench/shell-01/mutants.cjs` does.

**Second reading, because the scorer reads prose.** After the run a Sonnet reviewer marks H1–H7 and D1–D3 on every spec, the files copied under neutral names so the branch cannot be seen. The registered test uses the scorer's counts. On the usable path the primary test (H, K-after against K-before) is then computed on the reviewer's counts; "the same side" means both give p ≤ 0.05 with K-after above, or both do not. **If the two fall on different sides, nothing merges and the owner decides.** On the guard path, where the primary test is replaced by a median, the second reading is reported and is not a bar. Every disagreement is listed per session either way.

**Changes to the registered rules, made while building and before any session** (the registration allows this until step 2; each answers a gap the trial specs or the review exposed, none a session's data):
- **Sentences.** Most hazard groups now need **one sentence** of the block to hold their terms together (the registration said "one block carries all its term groups"); a sentence ends at a full stop, a question mark or an exclamation mark. H1 needs such a sentence (closed, with final / never changed / only resolved / the glossary) and, anywhere in the block, a word that the request departs from it (conflict, change, differs, the request, read as, rules …). A heading and the line straight under it are one block and one sentence; after a blank line they are two blocks.
- **H4** needs reopen, the organisation and a limiting word (only, limited, must, belong, verify, refuse …) in one sentence; a happy-path criterion naming "their own organisation" does not pass.
- **H5** is "the existing reopen is found": `reopenTicket` named, or `tickets.js` / `portal.reply` named with reopening in the same sentence. The registered "reused or extended" was dropped: naming the function is the evidence that the fact was looked up, and what to do with it is a design choice.
- **H6 also passes as a recorded assumption** (the table said "or a recorded decision"): the design's own rule for the case where nobody can answer turns a fifth question into a recorded assumption. Recorded means: the block stands under a heading holding "decision", "decided" or "assum", or the sentence that holds the SLA terms says so itself. A question with its recommendation inside a bullet outside any question section also passes.
- **H7**: a heading naming the exclusions with something under it that is not a placeholder (TBD, none, n/a), or a sentence naming one in at least three words beside the label; "does not cover", "won't build", "not included" count as labels.
- **Open questions.** (1) A heading can be one: a heading below a question section's heading that starts with a number or a question label, or holds a "?"; the blocks under it are not questions of their own, and its recommendation may stand in any of them. (2) A heading holding "assum", "decision", "out of scope" or "non-goal" **closes** the question section above it. (3) Under a question written as a paragraph, the list that follows holds its options, not further questions. (4) Numbered also means "Question 1", "OQ-1", "[Q1]"; leading emphasis, quote marks and emoji are ignored. (5) An answer line begins with Recommend, Default, Answer, Suggest (after "My"/"Our"/"The") or with an arrow or a tick, and is never a question. (6) Outside a question section a table row counts when one of its cells ends with "?".
- **A question's recommendation** is in its own text; for a table row, in the column whose header names one, when that cell is not empty; otherwise in the nested items and answer lines that follow it directly. The registered "or the block after it" let one question borrow the next one's answer.
- **Decoys.** Only the question is read, not the answer recommended after it. A decoy is a question holding the decoy's terms with a question word (which, what, how, is there, can …) and **no decision verb** (should, shall, would you, prefer) and none of the decision terms. The first build's "present state" condition is gone.
- **O2's suite run** drops the parent's test context from its environment (under `node --test` a nested run otherwise exits 0 whatever the fixture's tests do).
- **The runner's own "found" and "passed" columns** mix hazards, outcomes, form and reach and mean nothing for this task; only the tally's figures are read.
