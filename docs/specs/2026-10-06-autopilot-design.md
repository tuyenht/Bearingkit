## The quality gate: no step is done until all four hold

1. **It ran.** The suite green as its own command, a dry run where one exists, every "nothing found" check with a positive control. Fails: fix and run again. (For a change of documents only the suite still runs: tests read some of them.)
2. **A fresh, read-only reviewer per commit**, repeated until it reports no must-fix. The reviewer is always given the diff itself, the hard read-only rules, and the fixed meaning of must-fix (a statement that is false, a contradiction of a standing rule or of another file, a registered number changed, a change that does not do what it says); the author may add what to check, never what to conclude, and the reviewer's must-fix findings go into the log in its own words. **At most five rounds for one change, counted across split, amended or re-made commits; a fifth round with a must-fix sets the switch to `PAUSE`** (below) and the session stops. Nothing is committed "with a note that part of it was not read".
3. **A second review for the heavy steps.** The registration of a measurement, the record of its result, and a merge into `main` get, besides the Sonnet reviewer, a second reviewer with an adversarial brief and a fresh context, of a model other than the main session's where one is available (when none is, the same model with a fresh context, and the log says so). **A merge into `main` also goes to the advisor**, by the ladder in the owner's global instructions; if the advisor cannot be reached, the step waits and the switch goes to `PAUSE`. A change to this file outside the owner-held sections also goes to the advisor, asked exactly whether every edit only adds a stop, review or restriction and widens nothing a session may do; a "no", a doubt or an unreachable advisor makes it the owner's; raising a bound, removing a stop condition or a review, or touching the Autopilot line is the owner's.
4. **After a review, only the reviewer's own replacement text goes in.** A sentence the main session writes itself after a review is new text: it gets another round before the commit. A fix to a wrong sentence is made in every place that sentence is repeated.

The gate gives evidence, not proof: "perfect" cannot be shown. So every commit made under autopilot is logged with the number of review rounds it took and the must-fix findings of each (the log below), and the audit session reads that record.

## Control above the single step

- **The switch**: `docs/autopilot/state.md`, one word, `RUN` or `PAUSE`, with the caps. Every session reads it first; under `PAUSE` no session uses changes 1 to 6 below, and a session started without the owner does nothing but report. **A session only ever writes `PAUSE`; setting `RUN` is the owner's.** The `RUN` in the commit that adds `state.md` was written once by the session on message 3; that describes that commit and licenses nothing. No session writes `RUN` again, into an existing, missing or recreated file. The switch is read from `main`; missing, unreadable or any word but `RUN` is `PAUSE`. A `PAUSE` is committed alone, on `main` whatever branch the session was working on, and pushed (the switch and its line in the log, nothing else), so that the next start does not find the tree unclean. A session sets `PAUSE` when the gate fails (above), when an audit finds a fault, when a stop condition below is met, or when a registration's stop condition is met that this file gives no rule for; it then notifies the owner (the app's push notification when the tool is there; a notification that fails is itself written in the log) and writes why in the log.
- **Stop conditions at the start of a session.** First, before any work and any write, the lock: `docs/autopilot/.lock` (ignored by git; written at the start with the time, removed by the same session at its close). Found under six hours old: the session exits and writes nothing; found older: `PAUSE` and notify, and only the owner removes a stale one. Then, in this order, each a `PAUSE` with its reason: the working tree is not clean, or a bench or driver process is alive, and the newest handoff does not explain it; the newest handoff contradicts git on a branch head or on what is merged; three sessions in a row ended without a commit that passed the gate.
- **The decision log**: `docs/autopilot/decisions.md`. One entry for every decision a session takes that the owner used to take: what, by which rule of this file or of a registration, the option not taken, the commit, the review rounds. Each session closes with a log line: session number, gate-passed commits, sessions since the last audit. A stale lock counts as a session without a commit. At a start with four since the last audit, this session is the audit and does nothing else. The review rounds of a change still open and the number of scheduled sessions of the day are kept in the same log: nothing else spans sessions. The owner audits afterwards instead of approving beforehand.
- **The audit session**: a session of its own, after every fourth autopilot session and before every merge into `main`, its reading done by a fresh read-only sub-agent of a model other than the main session's (a session cannot change its own model; when no other model can be launched, said in the log). It reads the log and the diffs since the last audit, checks each logged decision against its rule, and re-checks one "nothing found" claim of its choice. A fault found: `PAUSE`.
- **Caps**: no measurement starts above 80% of the five-hour window (unchanged); **no autopilot work starts above 70% of the weekly limit**, read with `get_usage`: above it the session reports and stops; the context stop at 80% is unchanged.
- **Long runs**: a driver or bench command runs with the turn kept alive (the session waits on it in pieces of under ten minutes) instead of in the background after the turn ends; after each round the Windows System log is read for a sleep event (Kernel-Power 42, 107) inside it. A round with a sleep event inside is handled by the registration's own rule for cut and incomplete calls, and logged. This replaces, for these runs, the note of `docs/handoff/2026-09-17.md` on keeping the machine awake before a background measurement: the measurement no longer runs in the background.

## Decisions fixed before the data: what follows each outcome of a measurement

A registration made from now on carries these lines with its bars, so that no choice is made with the result in view. Applying them to the `plan-01` run of 2026-10-06, which was registered before them and whose result is known, waits for the same one-line confirmation (change 6).
- **Every bar holds on the first run of a text**: the registered guard runs, by rule. **The guard holds**: the gate's heavy-step review, the advisor, the audit session, then the merge into `main` by rule (change 4; until the owner has confirmed it, that merge is the owner's), logged. The daily install is not updated (the owner's).
- **A bar is missed on the first run of a text**: one second freeze is allowed, only if every edit is a general rule (no word of the fixture; no sentence that exists only to pass one hazard; the heavy-step reviewer is asked exactly that, edit by edit; the edit set is submitted once; no edit is re-worded and re-asked; one "no" ends the attempt and the measurement closes, the text unmerged on its branch, as a missed second run does). It is followed by **a run whose registration is identical to the first in task, fixture, rubric, readers, bars, numbers, model and analysis; only the text's commit and the sessions differ**. What the second run shows is said "on this task" and no wider. **A text that passes only on its second run is not merged by rule: that merge is the owner's**, since the edits were written after seeing which hazards failed.
- **A bar is missed on the second run**: the measurement closes, the text stays on its branch unmerged, and the work moves to the next item of the roadmap. **At most two runs of one skill's text on one task, ever; an edited text is not a "first run".**
- **Never by rule**: changing a bar, a number, a registered rule, the task, the fixture, the rubric, the readers or the model of a measurement after its data is known.
- **An incident a registration has no rule for** (as the misaligned reply of 2026-10-06 was): if every registered bar's verdict is the same under every candidate handling, the session takes the handling least favourable to the kit and logs all of them with their figures; otherwise `PAUSE`. The candidates are listed by the heavy-step reviewer, given only the registration and the incident; any handling it names that changes a verdict: `PAUSE`.

## Limits, said now

"No intervention" is not absolute: the machine off or asleep with the lid closed, the app closed, the weekly limit reached, or the measurement profile's login expired all stop the work, and only the last three leave a trace the owner sees at once. Three things still come to the owner by design: the one-line confirmation of changes 4, 5 and 6, a merge after a second run, and every `PAUSE`. A session that reviews its own kind of work can share its blind spots: the second reviewer, the advisor and the audit session narrow that and do not remove it; when the main session and the second reviewer are the same model, only the fresh context separates them. The pilot is one case. Whether the gate catches what matters is an open question that the log's counts are there to answer over time.
